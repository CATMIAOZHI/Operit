# Flight booking

## Plan the search

- Settle origin, destination, dates, travelers (children's ages), cabin and how flexible the user is. Assume the usual (one adult, one way) when unstated, and say so.
- Ask only for details that would change the route, price or traveler.

## Compare whole trips

- Search several sources: the airline's own site, Google Flights, Skyscanner, Kayak and regional sites. Some low-cost carriers sell mainly on their own sites.
- Rank by verified total price (taxes, fees, bags) together with total travel time. When the fastest and the cheapest differ, show both and say what the extra money or time buys.
- Compare whole itineraries only. Never set a one-way price against a round trip, or quietly change airport, date or cabin.
- For an exact flight number, book only an offer with that number.
- The price on the booking page is the price; search lists can be stale.

## Book

- Book where the verified fare is sold. Fill in travelers exactly as on their documents and have the user check.
- Orbit cannot pay for the user. Stop at the payment page and hand the browser to the user. If the price rises or terms get worse before payment, tell the user first.
- When a payment result is uncertain, check the order list or the confirmation message before booking again.
- Report the booking reference, flight numbers, local times, baggage allowance and change rules; add a check-in or departure reminder with orbit_task when useful.
